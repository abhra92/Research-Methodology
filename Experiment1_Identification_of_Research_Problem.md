## Step 5 — Per-Paper Summary (one paragraph each)

The numbering matches Step 4. Each summary states the problem, the method,
the data, the result, and the limitation the authors themselves acknowledge.

**A1.** Hybrid features + DQN + CNN. Achieves 99.75 % accuracy on a
specialised phishing dataset. **Limitation:** DQN training is unstable on
small datasets and the dataset is not standard, hampering reproduction.

**A2.** Compares DT, RF, and XGBoost on URL-based features (domain age,
HTTPS, lexical). Reports ROC-AUC and F1; ensemble models beat the single
tree. **Limitation:** URL-only features fail against image-heavy or
JS-rendered phishing pages.

**A3.** NLP over URL tokens with five classical classifiers (LR, SVM, NB,
RF, KNN). **Limitation:** URL-lexical features alone cannot catch phishing
sites that mimic a real brand's HTML.

**A4.** Text-based features with DNSPython + python-whois. **Limitation:**
WHOIS lookups are slow and privacy-restricted, making real-time deployment
difficult.

**A5.** TF-IDF + N-Gram vs SVM, statistically tested. **Limitation:** 84–88 %
accuracy is too low for production; the dataset may be imbalanced.

**A6.** ChatPhishDetector — uses an LLM over URL + HTML + screenshot. The
LLM returns a JSON decision with rationale. **Limitation:** LLMs are
expensive at request time; brand impersonation logic requires frequent
brand-list updates.

**B1.** CNN + classical ML on leaf / fruit images, with augmentation and
outlier detection. **Limitation:** Cross-dataset accuracy is not reported;
field-condition robustness is unknown.

**B2.** Depthwise CNN + SE blocks + residual skip. 98 % accuracy / 98.2 % F1.
**Limitation:** Tested on a curated dataset; field deployment (variable
lighting, mixed diseases) is not evaluated.

**B3.** Plain CNN on healthy vs diseased leaves. **Limitation:** No
comparison with transfer-learning baselines; small-scale dataset.

**B4.** CNN vs MobileNet with Grad-CAM XAI. CNN 89 %, MobileNet 96 %.
**Limitation:** XAI quality is qualitative, not measured against human
ground truth.

**B5.** Three-disease CNN. 95–98 % per disease. **Limitation:** Only three
diseases, no segmentation of disease region.

**B6.** Compares 17 CNN backbones; YOLOv7 best at 96.1 %. **Limitation:**
YOLOv7 is heavy; no edge / mobile deployment analysis.

**B7.** Systematic review of DL for plant disease. **Limitation:** Notes
the persistent issue of "small, imbalanced, lab-image datasets"; field
generalisation remains unsolved.

**B8.** SLR of ViT for plant disease. **Limitation:** Highlights that ViTs
require large annotated datasets and high compute — a barrier for
under-resourced settings.

**C1.** Survey of Arabic fake-news detection. **Limitation:** Most work is
English-centric; Arabic-specific resources are still scarce.

**C2.** Transformer over news content + social context. **Limitation:**
Requires social-context data that platforms often restrict.

**C3.** Emotion-driven multimodal transformer. **Limitation:** Emotion
labels are noisy; multimodal fusion is computationally heavy.

**C4.** BERT-based transformer, multi-domain corpus. **Limitation:** 95 %
accuracy is reported but the paper does not address LLM-generated
misinformation, which is the new threat.

**C5.** Adapts fake-news detection to the LLM era. **Limitation:** Mostly
English; cross-lingual transfer is hard.

**D1.** SoftPool CNN + multi-head BiGRU + Gram-matrix visualisations. Beats
SOTA on acc / F1 / FPR. **Limitation:** Heavy model; not edge-deployable on
low-power IoT nodes.

**D2.** DCNN on NF-ToN-IoT. Acc 0.9923, F1 0.9952. **Limitation:** Single
dataset; zero-day attack generalisation untested.

**D3.** Review of DL for IoT botnet. **Limitation:** Existing work
over-focuses on a single model; ensemble / hybrid DL is under-explored.

**D4.** XAI for IoT IDS. **Limitation:** Explanation quality vs human
trust is not formally measured.

**D5.** Image-of-traffic + CNN on TON IoT. 99.1 %. **Limitation:** PCAP →
image conversion is brittle to non-standard protocols.

**E1.** ML + blockchain blacklist. ANN best; real-time acc 72.49 %.
**Limitation:** Real-time accuracy much lower than offline; blockchain
latency hurts throughput.

**E2.** Systematic review. **Limitation:** Notes lack of standard
benchmarks and reproducibility code.

**E3.** DT, RF, NB, SGB, KNN on CICDDoS2019 subset. RF > 99 %. **Limitation:**
Down-sampling may hide minority-class performance.

**E4.** PCA + EDAD, LR/SVM/RF/KNN/DT. **Limitation:** PCA loses
interpretability of which features matter.

**E5.** RF, DT, AdaBoost, XGB, MLP, DNN with 22 features. RF 99.97 %.
**Limitation:** The paper admits class imbalance and feature-redundancy
issues despite Pearson selection.

**F1.** Comprehensive SA review. **Limitation:** Most DL models are
data-hungry; low-resource languages are not covered well.

**F2.** AeBERT-BGRU on IMDB / Polarity / OLID. **Limitation:** OLID
(offensive) and polarity datasets are not domain-matched; transfer is
fragile.

**F3.** BERT + Bi-LSTM + attention on Chinese tourist reviews. **Limitation:**
Single domain; code-mixed reviews are not handled.

**F4.** Fine-tuned BERT for SA. **Limitation:** As a preprint, peer-review
validation is pending.

**G1.** Eight ML models on PIMA + Cleveland. **Limitation:** Lifestyle
factors are added but not externally validated.

**G2.** RF on two Korean T2DM cohorts. **Limitation:** External validation
AUROC drops from 0.83 to 0.72 — generalisation concern.

**G3.** DT, XGB, RF, MLP on Kaggle heart-disease (73k). **Limitation:** 87 %
ceiling suggests unmodelled clinical features.

**G4.** SLR of ML + smart devices for HD / diabetes / obesity. **Limitation:**
30 studies, but most are non-randomised; risk of bias is high.

**H1.** CF + content + SVD hybrid. **Limitation:** Cold-start users still
not well handled.

**H2.** Content-based + TF-IDF + SVD. **Limitation:** No sentiment
modulation; only metadata similarity.

**H3.** Demographic + content + CF + sentiment. **Limitation:** Sentiment

**H4.** Web-crawled + static hybrid. **Limitation:** Crawling introduces

**I1.** Decision Tree for student prediction. **Limitation:** Single model

**I2.** Bagging / Boosting / Random Forest stacking on LMS data.

**I3.** EDM for early at-risk CS students. **Limitation:** Limited to CS

**J1.** Two-stage live-chat intent from browsing. **Limitation:** Privacy

**J2.** Description-augmented dataless intent. **Limitation:** Description
quality is the bottleneck.

**J3.** MINT-CL multi-turn intent. **Limitation:** Multilingual data

## Step 6 — Common Trends Across the Literature

Reading across the 25 papers, six cross-cutting trends emerge:

1. **Deep learning is now the default, but ensembles are coming back.** CNN,
   LSTM, BiGRU, BERT, and ViT appear in nearly every paper. Yet the
   highest reported numbers increasingly come from **stacked or hybrid
   ensembles** (RF + XGBoost, AdaBoost + RF, stacking of DL + classical).
2. **Transformer models have become the universal NLP backbone.** BERT,
   BiLSTM-attention, GPT-style LLMs appear in phishing, fake news, SA,
   intent classification, and even recommender systems.
3. **Explainability is now a stated requirement.** Grad-CAM, attention
   weights, LIME, and SHAP are appearing in plant disease, IoT IDS, and
   recommender papers.
4. **Public benchmark datasets dominate.** PlantVillage, MovieLens,
   CICDDoS2019, NF-ToN-IoT, PIMA, Cleveland, IMDB — these are reused
   everywhere, which is good for comparability but bad for novelty.
5. **Real-time / edge deployment is still under-served.** Models reach 99 %
   accuracy on a desktop GPU but are rarely tested on Raspberry Pi, mobile,
   or in-browser (TF.js / ONNX runtime).
6. **Multilingual and low-resource work is the new frontier.** Arabic fake
   news, Hindi/Bangla SA, Indic scripts, multilingual multi-turn intent are
   all flagged as under-explored.

## Step 7 — Limitations Acknowledged in the Literature

- **Dataset bias and imbalance.** Almost every paper reports ≥ 95 %
  accuracy, yet class imbalance is rarely handled with anything better than
  random undersampling.
- **Lack of real-world testing.** Lab datasets ≠ field conditions (plant
  disease, phishing, DDoS, fake news all suffer here).
- **Compute / latency mismatch with deployment.** Models are often too
  large for the device they should protect (IoT IDS, mobile phishing
  detection, on-device plant disease).
- **Reproducibility gaps.** Many papers do not release code, hyperparameters,
  or seeds; several reviews (B7, D3, E2) flag this explicitly.
- **English / Western data dominance.** Arabic, Indic, low-resource
  languages, and code-mixed text are under-served.
- **Static evaluation vs adaptive adversaries.** Phishing, DDoS, and fake
  news adversaries adapt; most models are evaluated on static snapshots.
- **Single-dataset validation.** A model that scores 99.9 % on CICDDoS2019
  often drops significantly on BoT-IoT or ToN-IoT — but cross-dataset
  evaluation is rare.

## Step 8 — Future Work Identified by the Authors

- **Hybrid and stacked models** that combine the interpretability of
  classical ML with the accuracy of DL.
- **Vision Transformers and self-supervised pretraining** for plant disease
  and medical imaging to reduce labelled-data needs.
- **XAI as a first-class evaluation metric**, not just a visual aid.
- **LLM-based agents** for phishing, fake-news triage, and chatbot intent
  understanding, with structured (JSON) output.
- **Edge / on-device deployment** of IDS, phishing, and plant-disease
  models (TF Lite, ONNX, quantization).
- **Cross-dataset / cross-domain evaluation** of IDS, DDoS, and fake-news
  detectors.
- **Multimodal and multilingual models** for fake news and recommender
  systems.
- **Privacy-preserving / federated learning** for healthcare and
  educational data mining.

## Step 9 — Research Gaps for a BCA Project

Distilling Steps 6–8, the following gaps are realistic for a one-semester
BCA project to address — i.e., doable on a single laptop, with public
datasets, and a clear evaluation protocol:

1. **No Indian / regional language work.** Most NLP papers (fake news, SA,
   intent) are English; Hindi / Tamil / Bengali are open.
2. **No browser-level / on-device implementation.** Phishing, IDS, plant
   disease are reported as research prototypes, not browser extensions or
   mobile apps.
3. **No explainability evaluation against users.** XAI is added visually
   but not measured against end-user trust or understanding.
4. **No cross-dataset benchmarking of "off-the-shelf" classical ML.**
   Sticking to RF / XGBoost on multiple public datasets in a single
   framework is publishable and missing.
5. **No comparative study on the same dataset with the same protocol
   across three families** (classical ML, CNN, transformer).
6. **No student-facing tool that operationalises EDM findings.** Most
   student-performance models stop at the metric; few are deployed as a
   usable dashboard for tutors.
7. **No Indian-context plant-disease or fake-news dataset evaluation.**
   Existing PlantVillage / Kaggle corpora are biased toward temperate
   crops and Western news.
8. **No reproducible end-to-end pipeline for IoT/DDoS IDS at the edge.**
9. **No hybrid recommender tested on Indian regional-language movie
   reviews.**
10. **No comparison between LLM zero-shot and a small fine-tuned model
    on the same task** in a BCA-achievable compute budget.

## Step 10 — Ten Research Problems

Each problem is fully specified with: Problem Statement, Motivation, Research
Gap, Research Question, Objectives, Hypothesis, Expected Outcome, Suitable
Research Method, Data Collection Method, and Statistical Analysis Method.

### Problem 1 — Hybrid Classical + Deep-Learning Phishing URL Detector for Indian Banking Customers

- **Problem Statement.** Phishing URLs targeting Indian banking customers
  frequently use Unicode look-alikes and regional-language lures that
  existing benchmark models (trained on UCI / Mendeley corpora) miss. A
  region-aware detector is needed.
- **Motivation.** Phishing is the most reported cybercrime in India (NCRP
  / I4C reports). Existing models generalise poorly to Indian-context
  URLs.
- **Research Gap.** Most papers (A1–A6) evaluate on global corpora; none
  reports an Indian-context evaluation.
- **Research Question.** Does a hybrid (lexical + character-level TF-IDF
  + 1-D CNN) model outperform a single-family baseline on an Indian
  banking-phishing dataset?
- **Objectives.**
  1. Collect ≥ 5 000 Indian-context URLs (legit + phishing).
  2. Engineer lexical, host-based, and character-N-gram features.
  3. Train and compare LR, SVM, RF, XGBoost, and a 1-D CNN.
  4. Report accuracy, precision, recall, F1, FPR, ROC-AUC.
- **Hypothesis.** The hybrid 1-D CNN will achieve ≥ 97 % F1 and lower
  FPR than any classical baseline on the Indian-context test set.
- **Expected Outcome.** An open-source Indian-phishing URL dataset and a
  reproducible notebook showing the gain.
- **Suitable Research Method.** Quantitative, experimental,
  benchmark-driven.
- **Data Collection Method.** PhishTank, OpenPhish, Alexa top sites,
  manual Indian banking URL collection; ground-truth labelling by
  CERT-In-style criteria.
- **Statistical Analysis Method.** 10-fold stratified CV; paired McNemar
  test between models; 95 % CI for F1.

### Problem 2 — Comparative Benchmark of CNN vs MobileNet vs Vision Transformer on Indian Crop Leaf Diseases

- **Problem Statement.** Published CNN models for plant disease are
  evaluated on PlantVillage (temperate crops). Indian crops (rice, wheat,
  mango, turmeric, chickpea) and Indian field conditions are under-tested.
- **Motivation.** Agriculture is ~18 % of Indian GDP; mis-classified
  disease costs yield. A field-deployable, light-weight model is needed.
- **Research Gap.** B1–B8 use PlantVillage; few include Indian crop
  diseases and few compare CNN / MobileNet / ViT under one protocol.
- **Research Question.** Which model family gives the best
  accuracy-vs-model-size trade-off for Indian crop leaf disease
  detection?
- **Objectives.**
  1. Build / curate an Indian-crop leaf dataset (≥ 10 classes).
  2. Train and evaluate CNN, MobileNetV3, EfficientNet-B0, and ViT-B/16.
  3. Compare accuracy, F1, parameter count, and inference time on a
     laptop CPU.
  4. Export the best model to TFLite.
- **Hypothesis.** MobileNetV3 will match CNN accuracy within 2 % while
  using ≥ 5× fewer parameters.
- **Expected Outcome.** A benchmark table and a TFLite model file.
- **Suitable Research Method.** Quantitative, experimental, comparative.
- **Data Collection Method.** PlantVillage (base) + ICAR-NRRI /
  IARI-published Indian-crop image sets + field photographs (with
  consent).
- **Statistical Analysis Method.** 5-fold CV; Friedman test across
  models; Nemenyi post-hoc; effect size (Cohen's d).

### Problem 3 — Hindi / Hinglish Fake-News Detector Using a Fine-Tuned mBERT vs a Classical Baseline

- **Problem Statement.** Most fake-news detectors (C1–C5) are
  English-only. Indian-language misinformation is rising sharply on
  WhatsApp / X / YouTube.
- **Motivation.** India is the largest WhatsApp market; vernacular
  misinformation directly affects elections, health, and communal
  harmony.
- **Research Gap.** No public Hindi / Hinglish fake-news benchmark with
  transformer-baseline comparison.
- **Research Question.** Does a fine-tuned multilingual BERT
  (mBERT / IndicBERT) significantly outperform a TF-IDF + SVM baseline on
  a Hindi / Hinglish fake-news dataset?
- **Objectives.**
  1. Assemble / curate a Hindi / Hinglish fake-news dataset.
  2. Train TF-IDF + SVM / LR and a fine-tuned mBERT / IndicBERT.
  3. Compare F1, accuracy, and inference time.
  4. Publish the dataset and code under a permissive licence.
- **Hypothesis.** mBERT / IndicBERT will achieve ≥ 90 % F1, beating
  TF-IDF + SVM by ≥ 8 F1 points.
- **Expected Outcome.** Open dataset + a reproducible notebook.
- **Suitable Research Method.** Quantitative, experimental.
- **Data Collection Method.** Public Hindi news corpora, manual labelling
  per IFCN code, scraping (rate-limited) from public fact-check sites.
- **Statistical Analysis Method.** 5-fold CV; paired bootstrap on F1;
  report 95 % CI.

### Problem 4 — Edge-Deployable IoT Intrusion Detection on a Raspberry Pi

- **Problem Statement.** Most IoT IDS (D1–D5) report > 99 % accuracy on
  a GPU. None evaluates real-time inference on a Raspberry Pi-class
  device.
- **Motivation.** Real IoT gateways are constrained; a model that runs
  at 5 fps with 95 % accuracy is more useful than one that scores 99 %
  on a GPU.
- **Research Gap.** No paper in our sample benchmarks an IDS on real
  edge hardware.
- **Research Question.** Can a quantized 1-D CNN run on a Raspberry Pi
  4 with ≥ 95 % F1 and ≤ 50 ms per flow?
- **Objectives.**
  1. Train a 1-D CNN on NF-ToN-IoT or CIC-IoT-DIAD 2024.
  2. Apply post-training quantization (TFLite int8).
  3. Measure latency, RAM, and F1 on Raspberry Pi 4.
  4. Compare with an RF baseline.
- **Hypothesis.** Quantized 1-D CNN ≥ 95 % F1, ≤ 50 ms per flow on
  Raspberry Pi 4.
- **Expected Outcome.** A reproducible pipeline + latency/F1 table.
- **Suitable Research Method.** Quantitative, experimental, performance
  benchmarking.
- **Data Collection Method.** Public NF-ToN-IoT / CIC-IoT-DIAD 2024
  dataset; captured traffic from a local testbed for validation.
- **Statistical Analysis Method.** 5-fold CV; mean ± std latency;
  Wilcoxon signed-rank between models.

### Problem 5 — Lightweight DDoS Detector for Campus Network

- **Problem Statement.** E1–E5 focus on enterprise / cloud DDoS; campus
  and small-office networks lack a deployable tool.
- **Motivation.** Indian college networks are frequent targets of
  student-launched DDoS (often with botnets).
- **Research Gap.** No public tool tuned for a campus-edge deployment.
- **Research Question.** Can a small Random Forest trained on
  CICDDoS2019 features detect attacks in a campus-edge router with
  ≤ 10 ms latency and ≥ 98 % F1?
- **Objectives.**
  1. Train RF, XGB, and a small MLP on CICDDoS2019 (≤ 20 features).
  2. Benchmark latency on a campus-edge hardware (laptop, mini-PC).
  3. Deploy as a SCAPY / nDPI plugin.
  4. Test on a 24-hour campus capture.
- **Hypothesis.** RF achieves ≥ 98 % F1 with ≤ 10 ms per flow on
  commodity hardware.
- **Expected Outcome.** Open-source SCAPY plugin + evaluation report.
- **Suitable Research Method.** Quantitative, experimental.
- **Data Collection Method.** CICDDoS2019 (training) + local
  24-hour PCAP (validation).
- **Statistical Analysis Method.** Stratified 5-fold CV; paired McNemar
  vs MLP.

### Problem 6 — Aspect-Based Sentiment Analysis for Indian E-Commerce Reviews

- **Problem Statement.** F1–F4 are coarse-grained (positive / negative).
  Indian e-commerce buyers want aspect-level insights (battery, delivery,
  packaging, value-for-money).
- **Motivation.** Indian vernacular reviews are aspect-rich; existing
  tools are English-only.
- **Research Gap.** No open aspect-based SA system tuned to Indian
  e-commerce reviews.
- **Research Question.** Does a fine-tuned BERT + CRF outperform a
  lexicon-based ABSA baseline on Indian e-commerce reviews?
- **Objectives.**
  1. Collect ≥ 3 000 labelled reviews per aspect from public sources.
  2. Fine-tune BERT-Base or XLM-RoBERTa.
  3. Compare to a VADER / lexicon baseline.
  4. Build a small demo web app.
- **Hypothesis.** BERT + CRF reaches ≥ 88 % aspect-F1 vs ≤ 70 % for the
  lexicon baseline.
- **Expected Outcome.** A demo web app + open dataset.
- **Suitable Research Method.** Quantitative, experimental.
- **Data Collection Method.** Public Amazon / Flipkart review pages
  (within ToS), labelled per SemEval-2014 ABSA scheme.
- **Statistical Analysis Method.** 5-fold CV; paired bootstrap on
  aspect-F1.

### Problem 7 — Heart-Disease Risk Prediction for Indian Cohorts Using Stacked Ensemble

- **Problem Statement.** G1–G4 are trained on PIMA (US / India mix) and
  Cleveland (US). Indian-specific risk-factor profiles (low HDL, high
  triglycerides) are not well modelled.
- **Motivation.** Cardiovascular disease is the leading cause of death
  in India; an India-specific model could enable better screening.
- **Research Gap.** No paper evaluates an Indian cohort on a
  reproducible stacked ensemble with explainability.
- **Research Question.** Does a stacked ensemble (DT + XGB + LR)
  outperform a single RF on an Indian heart-disease dataset, and does
  SHAP improve clinician trust?
- **Objectives.**
  1. Collect / curate an Indian heart-disease dataset (≥ 1 000 rows).
  2. Build stacked ensemble.
  3. Use SHAP to expose top features.
  4. Run a small user study with medical interns (n ≥ 10) to score
     trust on a Likert scale.
- **Hypothesis.** Stacked ensemble ≥ 90 % F1 and is rated as more
  trustworthy than a black-box DNN.
- **Expected Outcome.** Open dataset + model + SHAP dashboard.
- **Suitable Research Method.** Mixed methods (quantitative + small
  user study).
- **Data Collection Method.** Public UCI / Kaggle + a small
  (anonymised) hospital dataset under ethics approval.
- **Statistical Analysis Method.** Stratified 5-fold CV; paired t-test
  on Likert trust scores; bootstrap CI for F1.

### Problem 8 — Hybrid Movie Recommender with Sentiment-Aware Re-Ranking

- **Problem Statement.** H1–H4 are CF + content. None re-ranks
  recommendations using sentiment of recent reviews, which matters for
  newly released Indian films.
- **Motivation.** Indian OTT platforms need to surface newly released
  films accurately; sentiment on day-1 reviews is a strong signal.
- **Research Gap.** No open sentiment-aware re-ranker evaluated on
  MovieLens + IMDb.
- **Research Question.** Does a sentiment-aware re-ranker (BERT SA
  over recent reviews) improve NDCG@10 and user-style satisfaction
  metrics over a plain SVD baseline?
- **Objectives.**
  1. Use MovieLens-25M + IMDb / TMDb review scrapes.
  2. Build CF (SVD) baseline.
  3. Build sentiment-aware re-ranker with BERT.
  4. Evaluate NDCG@10, MRR, and a small user study.
- **Hypothesis.** Sentiment re-ranker improves NDCG@10 by ≥ 5 %.
- **Expected Outcome.** Open codebase + evaluation report.
- **Suitable Research Method.** Quantitative, experimental.
- **Data Collection Method.** MovieLens-25M + IMDb public datasets +
  limited, ToS-compliant scraping.
- **Statistical Analysis Method.** 5-fold CV on user splits; paired
  t-test on NDCG@10.

### Problem 9 — At-Risk BCA Student Predictor from LMS Logs

- **Problem Statement.** I1–I4 use static academic data. LMS
  click-stream / login / assignment-submission data is more timely.
- **Motivation.** Indian colleges run Moodle / Google Classroom; an
  early-warning model could improve pass rates.
- **Research Gap.** No published BCA-specific EDM model using
  LMS click-stream.
- **Research Question.** Does an ensemble (RF + XGBoost) on LMS
  click-stream features identify at-risk BCA students by week 4 of the
  semester with ≥ 85 % F1?
- **Objectives.**
  1. Get ethics approval + LMS data from one BCA department.
  2. Engineer per-week behavioural features.
  3. Train RF + XGB ensemble.
  4. Deploy as a Moodle plugin (read-only dashboard).
- **Hypothesis.** The ensemble reaches ≥ 85 % F1 by week 4.
- **Expected Outcome.** A non-intrusive dashboard + evaluation report.
- **Suitable Research Method.** Quantitative, experimental.
- **Data Collection Method.** Anonymised LMS logs (with consent under
  institute ethics).
- **Statistical Analysis Method.** Time-aware CV (rolling window);
  AUC-PR; SHAP feature importance.

### Problem 10 — Zero-Shot LLM vs Small Fine-Tuned Model for Chatbot Intent Classification (Multilingual)

- **Problem Statement.** J1–J3 use large LLMs or fine-tuned BERTs.
  Whether a small fine-tuned model can match a zero-shot LLM in a
  compute-restricted BCA setting is unclear.
- **Motivation.** Indian small businesses need cheap multilingual
  chatbots; large LLM API costs are prohibitive.
- **Research Gap.** No head-to-head, compute-cost-normalised
  comparison in the multilingual MTIC setting.
- **Research Question.** Can a small fine-tuned DistilBERT match
  zero-shot GPT-class LLM intent accuracy within 5 % while running
  in < 100 ms on a CPU?
- **Objectives.**
  1. Use a public multilingual MTIC benchmark (e.g. MultiAtis++ /
     MTIC-En-Hi or a public Chatbot Corpus).
  2. Fine-tune DistilBERT / mBERT.
  3. Run zero-shot LLM (locally, e.g. via Ollama) on the same
     test set.
  4. Compare accuracy, latency, and GPU-free inference.
- **Hypothesis.** DistilBERT matches zero-shot LLM within 5 % accuracy
  but is ≥ 20× cheaper per query.
- **Expected Outcome.** A reproducible benchmark + a deployable
  DistilBERT endpoint.
- **Suitable Research Method.** Quantitative, experimental.
- **Data Collection Method.** Public multilingual MTIC corpora.
- **Statistical Analysis Method.** McNemar's test; bootstrap CI on
  per-class F1; cost-per-1 000 queries.

## Closing Note for the Student

For a one-semester BCA project, **Problem 5 (campus DDoS detector)** is
the most realistic first choice: the dataset is large, the tools are
open-source, the metrics are standard, and the deliverable can be
demonstrated on the college's own network. **Problem 4 (edge IoT IDS)**
and **Problem 1 (Indian phishing URLs)** are equally strong runners-up,
and they share enough tooling that the literature review you write for
one is reusable for another.

Whatever you pick, frame the contribution as **"a small, reproducible,
region-aware evaluation"** rather than "a new SOTA model" — that is
exactly the gap the literature above leaves open, and it is the gap a
BCA project can honestly fill in one semester.
