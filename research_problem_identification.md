### 2.2 Frequently Used Methodologies

- **Surveys/questionnaires:** Dominant in cybersecurity awareness (B1, B3, B4, B6, B7, B8, B9, B10) and education (C5, C7, C9). Often uses validated instruments like HAIS-Q (B3).
- **Systematic literature reviews / surveys:** Common in AI-in-SE (A3, A7, A8, A9) and cybersecurity education (B2).
- **Case studies:** Used in AI-in-SE (A2) and cybersecurity (B9).
- **Empirical/experimental studies:** Used in education (C1, C9) and full-stack performance benchmarking (D5).
- **Model/framework development:** KAB model (B3), awareness scale development (B8).
- **Position/vision papers:** A1, C3, C10.

### 2.3 Datasets

- **HAIS-Q (Human Aspects of Information Security Questionnaire):** B3
- **Student survey responses:** B1, B4, B6, B7, B9, B10; C5 (889 students), C7 (40 students), C9
- **LLM benchmark datasets (implied in surveys):** A3, A7, A9 (code/test generation benchmarks)
- **Lighthouse/performance metrics:** D5
- **No standard public dataset** is consistently used across all four topics — most studies collect their own survey data.

### 2.4 Evaluation Metrics

- **Cybersecurity awareness:** Awareness scores, knowledge-attitude-behaviour measures, scale validation (Cronbach's alpha, factor analysis) — B3, B8
- **AI-in-SE:** Code generation accuracy, test coverage, pass@k (implied in surveys A3, A7, A9)
- **Education:** Student perceptions, learning outcomes, usage patterns, qualitative feedback — C1, C5, C7, C9
- **Full-stack:** Performance benchmarks, Lighthouse scores, response times — D5

### 2.5 Limitations (Common Across Papers)

1. **Small, non-representative samples** — Many cybersecurity and education studies use small or single-institution samples (e.g., 40 students in C7, 110 students in B6).
2. **Geographic/cultural specificity** — Results often limited to one country or region (Nigeria B10, Malaysia B6, Brazil C7, North Cyprus B5).
3. **Self-reported data** — Surveys rely on self-reporting, which may not reflect actual behaviour.
4. **Rapidly evolving technology** — LLM research becomes outdated quickly (A3, A7, A9).
5. **Lack of standardized datasets/metrics** — Makes cross-study comparison difficult.
6. **Thesis-based full-stack work** — Lacks peer review and rigorous methodology (D1–D10).
7. **Limited longitudinal studies** — Most are cross-sectional snapshots.

## Part 3: Research Gaps (Unsolved Problems)

1. **Context-specific cybersecurity awareness in developing regions** — Most studies focus on Western/Asian contexts; there is a gap in assessing awareness among BCA/undergraduate students in India/South Asia with validated, locally-adapted instruments.

2. **Awareness vs. actual behaviour gap** — Many studies measure awareness (knowledge) but few rigorously measure whether awareness translates into protective behaviour, especially among non-CS students.

3. **Longitudinal impact of AI assistants on learning** — Most education studies are short-term; there is a gap in measuring the long-term effect of AI assistants (e.g., ChatGPT, Copilot) on programming skill development and academic integrity.

4. **AI-assisted learning for specific SE skills** — Little research on using AI tutors for non-coding SE skills (requirements, design, testing, soft skills) at the undergraduate level.

5. **Standardized, peer-reviewed full-stack performance benchmarks** — The full-stack topic lacks rigorous, peer-reviewed comparative studies; most are theses with ad-hoc benchmarks.

6. **Security awareness in AI-assisted development** — As developers increasingly use AI code generators, there is a gap in studying whether this affects security awareness and secure coding practices among students.

7. **Effective educational interventions** — Few studies design and evaluate specific interventions (training modules, gamified awareness programs) with pre/post testing; most only measure current awareness levels.

8. **Cross-institutional comparative studies** — Little work compares cybersecurity awareness or AI adoption across multiple institutions with standardized instruments.

9. **Explainability and trust in AI code assistants** — Students' trust, over-reliance, and ability to verify AI-generated code is under-researched.

10. **Integration of cybersecurity into software engineering curricula** — A gap in how to effectively embed security awareness into BCA/SE programs rather than as a standalone topic.

## Part 6: Best Research Problem Recommendation

### Recommended: **"Cybersecurity Awareness and Behaviour Among BCA Students in India: A Survey-Based Study"**

### Why this is the best choice:

1. **High feasibility for a BCA undergraduate** — It requires only a well-designed survey, basic statistical analysis (descriptive statistics, correlation), and no advanced programming or ML skills. This matches the skill level of a BCA student.

2. **Excellent data availability** — The researcher's own institution provides a ready, accessible sample of BCA students. No external datasets or expensive tools are needed.

3. **Clear research gap** — As shown in the analysis (Part 3, Gap #1), most cybersecurity awareness studies focus on Western, Middle Eastern, or Southeast Asian contexts. There is a genuine gap in peer-reviewed studies on **Indian BCA students** using a validated, locally-adapted instrument.

4. **Reasonable time frame** — A survey-based study can be completed within a single academic semester (data collection + analysis + write-up), fitting the Research Methodology course timeline.

5. **Publication potential** — The topic is well-represented in journals like *Education and Information Technologies*, *Applied Sciences*, and *IEEE Access* (see B1, B3, B9). A well-executed, context-specific study with a validated instrument has a realistic chance of being published or presented at a student conference.

6. **Builds on existing validated instruments** — The researcher can adapt the HAIS-Q (used in B3) or the personal cybersecurity awareness scale (B8), giving methodological rigor without needing to develop a new instrument from scratch.

7. **Practical relevance** — The findings can inform cybersecurity education interventions at the researcher's own institution, giving the project real-world value.

### Suggested research questions:
- RQ1: What is the current level of cybersecurity awareness among BCA students?
- RQ2: Is there a gap between cybersecurity knowledge and protective behaviour?
- RQ3: What demographic/educational factors (e.g., year of study, prior training) are associated with higher awareness?

### Suggested methodology:
- **Design:** Cross-sectional survey
- **Instrument:** Adapted HAIS-Q or validated awareness scale
- **Sample:** 100–200 BCA students at the researcher's institution
- **Analysis:** Descriptive statistics, correlation, group comparisons (t-test/ANOVA)
- **Ethics:** Institutional ethics approval, informed consent, anonymized data
